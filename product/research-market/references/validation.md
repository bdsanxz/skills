# Limits and validation

What posts on a social network cannot show, and how the user can check a finding before acting on it. Use it to write the last section of the report.

## Biases to name in the report

Name the ones that apply to this run, each with what was done about it.

| Bias | What it does | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who is there | The people who post skew urban, young to middle-aged and comfortable online. | Say which audience the sample can speak for. Recommend other sources for the rest. |
| Extremes | People post when they are very angry or very pleased. | Rank themes by how many posts and authors share them, not by how loud they are. |
| Virality | The top tab favours posts that spread. | Compare with the latest tab. A theme found only in top posts may rest on one of them. |
| The queries | The search words decide what is found. | Use synonyms and several wordings. List the queries in the report. |
| Complaint channels | Posts sent to a support account are mostly failures only that company can fix. | Balance them with topic queries about wishes, behaviour and alternatives. |
| Recency | Search returns far more recent posts than old ones. | Do not read a rising count in the last days as a trend. |
| Promotion and bots | Paid and automated posts imitate real voices. | Leave them uncoded. Exclude the accounts in the next collection. |
| Confirmation | Evidence for the expected answer is easier to see. | Run queries that could contradict it, and code those posts too. |
| Lost context | Sarcasm and jokes read like statements. | Read the post that was replied to. |

## Hypotheses to validate

Every finding is a hypothesis until it is checked outside the network it came from. For each top theme and each top opportunity, write the hypothesis in one sentence and the cheapest check that could prove it wrong:

- **Other places the audience talks**: app store and marketplace reviews, Reddit and local forums, YouTube, TikTok and Instagram comments, community groups. Does the same theme appear there, in the same words?
- **Search data**: do people search for the problem or the workaround, and is that rising or falling?
- **Interviews**: five to ten people from the target segment.
- **A survey**: how common is each theme among people who never post about it?

## Interview questions

Ask about what happened, not about what someone would do:

- "Tell me about the last time you ran into this. What happened?"
- "What have you tried? Why did you stop?"
- "What made you start looking for a solution at that moment?"
- "How did you choose that one over the others?"
- "How much have you spent on this so far, in money and in time?"
- "If this were solved, what would change for you?"

Ask "why" two or three times to reach the real motive. Offer the user questions written for their themes, using the audience's own words from the language dictionary.
