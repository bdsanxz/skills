# Multi-reference composition

Several uploads — people, products, places, logos, layouts — are combined into one coherent result: a single scene, a campaign board, a split composition, a page. The craft is giving each image a number and a job, deciding where each element goes, and unifying light, scale and style so the result reads as one piece. To borrow only a quality (style, pose, palette) from a reference, see `reference-images.md`.

Entries with `[SLOTS]` list an example value for each slot.

## M12 · Cinematic keyframe contact sheet

`1–2 uploads: character, location` · `storyboard sheet` · `16:9`

*Ideas in play:* a role, continuity rules, a beat plan and a labeled grid, all in one master image.

*Slots:* `[LOGLINE]` a courier must deliver a sealed letter across a flooded city before midnight · `[LENGTH]` 30 seconds · `[ANCHORS]` the courier's yellow raincoat, the sealed red-wax letter, constant drizzle, sodium-orange street light, a teal-and-amber grade

```text
Role: you are a trailer director, cinematographer and storyboard artist.

Image 1 is the main character; Image 2, if provided, is the main location. From them, plan a [LENGTH] sequence: [LOGLINE]. Structure it in four beats: setup, build, turn, payoff.

Continuity rules: the same character, wardrobe, location, time of day, lighting and color grade in every frame. Do not introduce new main characters. Visual anchors that never change: [ANCHORS].

Output ONE master image, landscape 16:9: a 3×3 contact sheet of nine keyframes in story order. Vary shot types across the sheet: an extreme wide establishing shot, medium shots, a close-up on the hands and the letter, an over-the-shoulder, a low-angle hero shot, and a final wide. Shallow depth of field in close-ups, deeper in wide shots. Each panel is labeled in its bottom margin, outside the image, with its keyframe number, shot type and duration in small white type on black, for example "KF1 · EXTREME WIDE · 4s" — never covering the picture. No other text.
```
